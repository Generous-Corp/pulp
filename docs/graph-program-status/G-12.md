Real-time scheduling (2026-11-14): the lead kept the wake rule (p99 below 0.1
sample) and asked for real-time callback scheduling in the shared matrix
harness. Every cell's callback thread, including the G-12 activation cell, now
requests a Mach time-constraint policy (SCHED_FIFO on Linux), records the class
read back from the kernel in the receipt, and refuses rather than falling back
when the request is denied. Details, tests and negative controls are in
[`paced-timing-rt-scheduling-20261114.md`](../evidence/paced-timing-rt-scheduling-20261114.md).

obtained `time_constraint` in 48/48 cells, cut the median p50 wake from about
440 us to 8 us, and recorded 0 deadline misses against 20 at default
scheduling. It does not meet the wake bar: the best cell's p99 wake was 0.48
sample, against 0.1. No quiet-host pilot was run, because the host's 1-minute
load (25 to 31) was far above the admission limit of 7.

Shared wake driver (2026-11-16): the wake bar stays at p99 absolute start
error below 0.1 sample. Both paced paths, the matrix cell harness
(`test/harness/paced_timing_cell.hpp`) and the activation driver in
`test/fixtures/g5_graph_event_rt_cases.inc`, now wait through one fixed-origin
deadline driver, `test/harness/paced_deadline_driver.hpp`, instead of
`sleep_until(target)`:

- Targets are `origin + i * frames * 1e9 / rate` ns in integers. A late
  callback keeps its target; the driver never moves the origin or a later
  target, so lateness stays in the recorded start error.
- The default `deadline-spin` backend waits on an absolute kernel deadline
  (`mach_wait_until` on macOS, `clock_nanosleep` with `TIMER_ABSTIME` on
  Linux) until a guard before the target, then spins on the measurement clock
  to the target. The guard is calibrated on the callback thread after its
  real-time class is applied (nearest-rank p99 overshoot of 64 kernel waits of
  200 us, plus 10 us), clamped to `[20 us, clamp(period / 4, 20 us, 2 ms)]`.
  The spin is bounded (default `2 x guard + 10 us`); a spin past its bound
  falls back to a kernel wait and is recorded as an overrun. Kernel-wait
  retries after the first return share that bound and are counted in the spin
  CPU cost, so a wait that keeps returning at once cannot busy-wait unseen.
- The kernel deadline is mapped from the measurement clock once per driver.
  The mapping brackets the native clock read between two measurement-clock
  reads, retries up to 16 times for a bracket within 20 us, and records the
  narrowest bracket as `clock_mapping_skew_ns`.
- Each callback records the kernel wake, the callback start and the thread CPU
  time the spin used, separately. The CPU read sits after the start timestamp,
  so it never delays the recorded start; its sub-microsecond cost lands in the
  callback duration.
- `sleep-until` keeps the old wait as the A/B baseline. It never qualifies.
- These waits are test-driver code only; no production DSP path waits like
  this.

The receipt schema is now `pulp.graph_paced_timing_matrix_receipt.v3` (raw
cell format `pulp_paced_timing_cell_v2`). It adds the wake request and rule,
each cell's printed wake settings (backend, kernel wait, guard and its
source, bound and clamp flag, spin limit, calibration percentiles, Mach
timebase, thread CPU clock resolution, injected lateness, kernel wait
failures, clock mapping skew), and per-cell kernel-wake, spin-wall and spin-CPU percentiles with
late-entry, kernel-overshoot and spin-overrun counts, all recomputed from the
raw rows by `validate`. New disqualifiers: `wake_backend_not_deadline`,
`injected_lateness`, `spin_overrun:<cell>`, `wake_wait_failed:<cell>` and
`clock_mapping_skewed:<cell>` (mapping bracket wider than 20 us).

The G-12 activation receipt is now `pulp.graph_paced_activation_receipt.v2`:
its `harness_payload_bytes` includes the per-callback wake records
(`wake_stride` x callbacks, reported as `wake_bytes`) beside the rows, audio
and I/O buffers, and `validate` checks the complete sum.
`--inject-late-us N --inject-late-every K` is the injected-lateness negative;
`--planted-sleep-us` remains the overrun negative. A no-op fixture,
`noop-wake` (`Paced no-op wake cell`, never qualifying), isolates the wake
path for the A/B pilot.

What this proves today: only that the driver and receipt behave as specified
under the scripted-clock unit tests and the self-tests. A 32-callback smoke
over the 12 cells at 128 frames on the loaded m3 builder (1-minute load about
24, not admitted) met the per-cell thresholds in 12/12 cells, with the kernel
waking ahead of every target. That is far too short and too loaded to say
anything about the bar. No pilot was run: the host's 1-minute load was above
7 throughout.
